STYLE (every image): the look of a classic late-1990s Korean 2D online fantasy RPG, remastered in high resolution
- A cosy, nostalgic, gentle and cute online fantasy world full of friendly adventurers: 2D character sprites walking over hand-painted 3D fields, seen from about 45 degrees above.
- Everything looks like classic hand-pixelled 2D game sprites redrawn cleanly at high resolution (smooth, not blocky): small, precise shapes; soft 3- or 4-tone shading per material with a light dithered texture; thin outlines in a darker shade of each colour (dark brown on skin and leather, dark navy on blue cloth), never thick black lines.
- NOT a modern mobile-game look: no glossy plastic rendering, no airbrushed gradients, no thick outlines, no 3D render, no flat vector style.
- Colours: a muted, slightly desaturated medieval-fantasy palette (dusty navy, slate blue, khaki, leather brown, ivory, moss green, burgundy, old brass), soft warm daylight from the upper left.
- Every design is ORIGINAL. Do not copy any existing game's characters, monsters, items, logos or interface, and do not use any existing game's name.
- Character proportions like the sprites of that era: about 3 heads tall: a big head with spiky, layered manhwa-style anime hair, large almond-shaped eyes with one bright highlight, a small pointed nose and a small mouth; a slim body with fairly long legs, small hands and feet. NOT a round, chubby, super-deformed chibi.
- Outfits are practical, simple adventurer gear with a few clear colour blocks, like the beginner classes of an old online RPG, not ornate.

Rules for every RIG POSE image (portrait 2:3):

Rules for every PARTS SHEET (landscape 3:2):
- Every part is drawn COMPLETE, as if the parts in front of it had been removed, including areas normally hidden underneath (the shoulder under the pauldron, the top of the thigh under the tunic).
- Joint ends are rounded like ball joints and run about 15% past the joint, so bending never shows a gap.
- Each part keeps exactly the orientation it has in the rig pose (do not rotate parts to fit) and exactly the SAME SCALE as the rig pose (do not enlarge small parts).
- No shadows cast by other parts; each part is lit on its own.
- Parts arranged in a neat grid in the listed order (left to right, top to bottom), with plenty of empty background around every part; nothing touching or overlapping; nothing touching the image edge; each part exactly once.
- FACES rules for the head sheets: each EYE piece is BOTH eyes together with their eyebrows, spaced and angled exactly as they sit on the blank head (three-quarter view, so the far eye is slightly narrower), sized to fit it. Eyes, in order: 1 open, calm; 2 half-closed (mid-blink); 3 closed (soft downward arcs); 4 happy closed (upward arcs); 5 focused attack (narrowed, brows angled down); 6 hurt (squeezed shut); 7 knocked out (spirals); 8 surprised (wide, small pupils). Mouths, in order, sized to fit the head: 1 small smile; 2 neutral closed; 3 open happy (cheer, tongue visible); 4 determined tight line; 5 battle shout (open, teeth); 6 ouch (wavy); 7 small "O".
